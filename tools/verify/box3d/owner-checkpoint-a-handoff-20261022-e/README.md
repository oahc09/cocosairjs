## Ownership and terminal failure semantics

- Pure component identity/kind/Node/duplicate checks run before native createBody. A real component cannot be registered again through another adapter. Actual RigidBody and Collider classes are checked; node-bearing plain objects are rejected.
- Successful native body construction establishes one logical body. Separate adapter records identify actual components; disabling a collider does not remove its identity or material from the temporary fence roster.
- Step snapshots and original component/body list are frozen before the operation. Union mutation leases cover actual System, Node.prototype, owned body Nodes and ancestors, actual components (including disabled ones), observed collider materials and default material. Union transform leases cover the Node ancestry. `finally` releases both.
- Preflight rejection precedes the world stepping/native region and remains recoverable. A completed native step followed by an uncaught writeback mutation refusal faults without losing known completion. Reads are allowed in committed writeback/dispatch phases and rejected during solving/fault/close; configuration is refused throughout temporary scopes.
- Failed component disable does not claim a successful disabled transition. Unknown body destruction leaves ownership for the unique world teardown.
- Successful native world destruction clears logical body records. Unknown/failed native world destruction terminates JS fences/queue but retains body identities with `nativeOwnership: 'unresolved'` and `unresolvedNativeBodies`. Subsequent JS component cleanup can reduce components to zero without reducing that unresolved body count. In the explicit mock failure case, one native body remains unresolved and the preparation ledger correctly ends `cleanup-failed`; this case is **not** reported as released native resources.
- The destroy/disable injections are explicitly artificial mock failures, not natural defects in Box3D. The test named “not retried body destruction” counts entry into the original mock implementation, not all calls to its throwing replacement. The stricter absence of replay follows the terminal source branches and must still be checked by a complete real-binding call recorder in subsequent integration.
